Vietnam & Australia · Evidence base, calendar year 2025

Two economies that trade enormously and invest in each other barely at all.

II. What is the current state of relations?

Three pillars carry the relationship — trade, investment, and people and labour. The first is strong but narrow, the second has gone backwards, and the third is real but capped. Before the pillars, one finding frames all three.

For every dollar of annual two-way trade, the two economies hold about 6.8 cents of investment in each other. They would need to multiply mutual investment by nearly 15× to hold as much capital in each other as they exchange in a single year.

Two ledgers, one relationship

Canberra and Hanoi do not report the same relationship. The disagreement is methodology, not error — and being able to explain it is worth more marks than quoting either number alone.

Why the two accounts differ

Services in, services out. The Australian figure covers goods and services. Education-related travel (A$2,658m) and recreational travel (A$2,993m) are nearly A$5.7bn the Vietnamese merchandise account never sees.
Opposite trade balances. On the Australian ledger, Australia runs a deficit. On the Vietnamese merchandise ledger, Vietnam runs a deficit. Both are true — services flip the sign.
Origin versus consignment. Australia records imports by country of origin; goods made in Vietnam but shipped via third-party hubs are attributed differently on each side.
Stock versus registered capital. ABS measures a balance-of-payments stock — what is currently owned, net. Vietnam counts cumulative registered capital since 1988, which never nets out an exit. The Vietnamese figure is about the Australian one.
Currency and period. Australian figures are A$; Vietnamese figures are US$. Neither the exchange rate nor the reference period is held constant.

Vietnam's FDI market in 2025 — and Australia's absence from it

Why this is not simply "Australia lost interest"

Third-country routing. Much Australian capital enters Vietnam through Singapore or Hong Kong holding structures. Balance-of-payments statistics attribute it to the immediate counterparty, so it vanishes from the Australia–Vietnam line.
Small-number volatility. At this scale one divestment or fund restructure moves the whole aggregate. ABS also suppresses the FDI component of Vietnamese investment in Australia entirely, marking it "not published".
Different concept, different answer. Vietnam's ministry counts cumulative registered capital and reports roughly double the ABS figure. Neither is wrong; they measure different things.

IV. Strong in flows, weak in stocks

Method & sources

Where every number came from

Every figure is either read directly from a cited public source or computed from those figures by scripts/build_data.py, which runs consistency checks before the page is built. No value is estimated or illustrative.

Reading the data critically

Benchmark years, not a continuous series. The investment chart has four observations (2018, 2019, 2022, 2025). Connecting lines are for readability; the path between points was not observed.
Stocks are volatile at this scale. A two-billion-dollar bilateral position is small enough that one corporate transaction moves it by tens of percent. Read year-on-year changes as direction, not precision.
Confidentiality suppression. ABS marks several FDI components "not published". Where that applies, this page shows total investment rather than inventing an FDI split.
Top-six lists are not totals. DFAT publishes only the six largest items per direction; those cover 71.4% of Australian exports but only 53.3% of imports, so the two directions are not equally well described by their top lines.
Two currencies, two concepts. Australian figures are A$, goods and services; Vietnamese figures are US$, merchandise only. This page never adds one to the other.
2026 items are contemporaneous reporting. The August 2026 state visit, the US$20bn target and the US$2bn registered-investment milestone are news reporting, not audited statistics, and are kept separate from the 2025 statistical base.

Images, flags and insignia

The flags are the canonical public-domain SVGs, so the Union Jack counterchange, the Commonwealth Star and the Southern Cross are geometrically correct rather than approximated. Photographs are official or Creative Commons licensed and are attributed individually below and beside each image; scripts/fetch_media.py downloads them and regenerates the credits from the licence metadata, refusing any file whose licence is not on its allow-list.

On national emblems. This page deliberately does not display the Commonwealth Coat of Arms or the National Emblem of Viet Nam. Both are protected state insignia rather than ordinary logos: the Australian Government's published guidelines restrict use of the Commonwealth Coat of Arms to Commonwealth entities and prohibit uses that imply official endorsement, and Viet Nam's national emblem is regulated in the same spirit. Since this page analyses and criticises both governments' economic policy, carrying their insignia would imply an endorsement that does not exist. National flags carry no such restriction, which is why they are used instead. The institutional marks appropriate to this work are those of the Diplomatic Academy of Vietnam and Macquarie University, which appear on the presentation deck itself.

Reproducing this page

data/dataset.json is the single source of truth. Running python3 scripts/build_data.py validates it — two-way trade must equal exports plus imports, top-six items must not exceed their totals, partner shares must be ordered and bounded, and every source reference must resolve — then derives the computed statistics and writes assets/data.js. If a check fails, the build fails and the page is not rebuilt. python3 scripts/make_slide_charts.py renders the deck-coloured chart images in docs/slide-charts/.
